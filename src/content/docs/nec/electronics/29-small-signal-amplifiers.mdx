## 1. The idea

Bias sets a quiet Q-point (lesson 28). Now feed in a small AC signal:

- An **input coupling capacitor** lets the AC signal into the base but blocks DC, so the source doesn't upset the bias.
- The signal wiggles V_BE slightly → I_B wiggles → I_C wiggles β times more → the voltage across R_C wiggles a lot.
- An **output coupling capacitor** passes the amplified AC to the load and blocks the DC collector voltage.
- A **bypass capacitor** across R_E shorts it for AC, so the emitter is at AC ground — giving much more gain while keeping R_E's DC stability.

"Small signal" means small enough that the transistor behaves almost linearly around the Q-point, so we can use simple linear models.

## 2. The r_e model

For small signals, the base–emitter junction looks like a small resistance:

`r_e = 26 mV / I_E`  (at room temperature)

**CE amplifier, emitter bypassed:**
- Voltage gain **`A_v ≈ −R_C / r_e`** (with a load: −(R_C ∥ R_L)/r_e). The minus sign = **180° phase inversion**.
- Input impedance ≈ R1 ∥ R2 ∥ β r_e.
- Output impedance ≈ R_C.

**Worked example (lesson 28's circuit).** I_E = 1.41 mA → r_e = 26/1.41 ≈ **18.4 Ω**.
A_v ≈ −2,200/18.4 ≈ **−120**. Input impedance ≈ 47k ∥ 10k ∥ (100 × 18.4 = 1.84k) ≈ **1.5 kΩ**.

**Same circuit, R_E not bypassed:** A_v ≈ −R_C/(r_e + R_E) ≈ −2,200/1,018 ≈ **−2.2**. Much less gain, but very stable and predictable (negative feedback, lesson 32).

## 3. h-parameters (hybrid model)

The transistor is treated as a two-port box described by four "hybrid" parameters (CE versions shown):

| Parameter | Meaning | Typical CE value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **h_ie** | input impedance (output shorted) | ≈ β r_e, about 1–2 kΩ |
| **h_fe** | forward current gain (output shorted) | ≈ β, 50–300 |
| **h_re** | reverse voltage ratio (input open) | ≈ 10⁻⁴ (usually ignored) |
| **h_oe** | output admittance (input open) | ≈ 10–25 µS (1/h_oe ≈ 50 kΩ) |

"Hybrid" because the units are mixed: ohms, a pure ratio, a pure ratio, siemens. With h_re and h_oe ignored, the approximate model is just h_ie at the input and a current source h_fe·i_b at the output — the same as the r_e model.

## 4. Frequency response

Plot gain against frequency (log scale) for a typical RC-coupled amplifier:

- **Mid-band:** flat — the capacitors act as shorts and the transistor is fast enough.
- **Low frequencies:** coupling and bypass capacitors' reactance (1/2πfC) becomes large, so gain falls. The **lower cut-off f_L** is where gain is down to 0.707 (−3 dB).
- **High frequencies:** tiny internal junction capacitances (and stray wiring capacitance) start to short the signal; the **Miller effect** multiplies the base–collector capacitance by the gain. Gain falls again; that's the **upper cut-off f_H**.
- **Bandwidth** = f_H − f_L (≈ f_H when f_L is small).
- **Gain–bandwidth product** is roughly constant for a given transistor: more gain, less bandwidth.

## 5. Multistage amplifiers

One stage rarely gives enough gain, so stages are **cascaded**:

- Total gain = product of stage gains (or the **sum** in dB).
- **RC coupling:** cheapest and most common; doesn't amplify DC.
- **Transformer coupling:** impedance matching; bulky; used in power and RF stages.
- **Direct coupling:** passes DC (used inside ICs and op-amps).
- Overall bandwidth is **narrower** than one stage's.

**Example.** Two stages of −50 and −40: total gain = +2,000 (two inversions cancel) = 20 log 2,000 ≈ **66 dB** (34 dB + 32 dB).

**Question.** Draw a single-stage RC-coupled CE amplifier and explain its frequency response.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**Circuit.** A voltage-divider-biased CE stage (R1, R2, R_C, R_E) with input coupling capacitor C_in, output coupling capacitor C_out and emitter bypass capacitor C_E. *(Draw.)*

**Operation.** The bias sets the Q-point in the active region. The AC input, coupled by C_in, varies the base current; the collector current varies β times more, producing an amplified, 180° phase-inverted voltage across R_C, coupled to the load by C_out. C_E bypasses R_E for AC to maintain high gain. Mid-band gain ≈ −(R_C ∥ R_L)/r_e.

**Frequency response.** *(Sketch gain vs log f: rising, flat, falling.)*
1. *Low-frequency region:* the reactances of C_in, C_out and C_E increase, so signal is lost and feedback through un-bypassed R_E rises; gain falls below f_L.
2. *Mid-band:* capacitors act as shorts and internal capacitances as opens; gain is constant and maximum.
3. *High-frequency region:* junction capacitances (C_be, C_bc, magnified by the Miller effect) and stray capacitances shunt the signal, and β falls; gain drops above f_H.

**Bandwidth.** f_L and f_H are the −3 dB (half-power) points; BW = f_H − f_L.

**Application.** Audio and general-purpose voltage amplification in multistage amplifiers.
