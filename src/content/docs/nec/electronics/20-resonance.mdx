**Goal:** find the resonant frequency, say what happens to impedance, current and power factor at resonance (series and parallel), and use Q and bandwidth.

## 1. Series RLC

Put R, L and C in series. The inductor's voltage points one way (+90°), the capacitor's the opposite way (−90°), so their reactances **subtract**:

`X = XL − XC`   `|Z| = √(R² + (XL − XC)²)`

- XL > XC → net inductive → current lags.
- XC > XL → net capacitive → current leads.

## 2. Resonance

XL grows with frequency and XC shrinks, so at exactly one frequency they're equal and **cancel**:

`XL = XC` → `2πf0L = 1/(2πf0C)` → **`f0 = 1 / (2π√(LC))`**

At series resonance:

- |Z| = **R** — its **minimum**; the circuit looks purely resistive.
- Current = V/R — its **maximum**.
- Power factor = **1** (voltage and current in phase); power drawn is maximum.
- The voltages across L and C are **equal and opposite** and can each be far bigger than the supply: `VL = VC = Q × V` (**voltage magnification**). Series resonance is called an **acceptor** circuit.

## 3. Quality factor and bandwidth

**Q** measures how sharp the peak is:

`Q = ω0L / R = (1/R) √(L/C)`

**Bandwidth** is the range of frequencies between the two **half-power points**, where the current has fallen to Imax/√2 (0.707 Imax) and power to half:

`BW = f2 − f1 = f0 / Q`

Smaller R → higher Q → **taller, narrower** peak → more **selective**. Bandwidth is **inversely** proportional to Q.

**Worked example.** L = 0.1 H, C = 10 µF, R = 20 Ω, V = 10 V (the lab's default circuit).
- f0 = 1/(2π√(10⁻⁶)) = 1/(2π × 10⁻³) ≈ **159 Hz**.
- Imax = 10/20 = **0.5 A**.
- Q = (1/20) × √(0.1/10⁻⁵) = (1/20) × 100 = **5**.
- BW = 159/5 ≈ **32 Hz**.
- VL = VC = 5 × 10 = **50 V** — on a 10 V supply.

## 4. Parallel resonance (the "tank" circuit)

L and C in parallel (with R) resonate at the same f0 = 1/(2π√LC) for the ideal case, but behave the opposite way:

- Impedance is **maximum**.
- Line current is **minimum**, while a large current circulates between L and C (**current magnification**, Q × I).
- Power factor = **1**.
- Called a **rejector** circuit.

| At resonance | Series | Parallel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impedance | minimum (= R) | maximum |
| Line current | maximum | minimum |
| Power factor | unity | unity |
| Magnifies | voltage (Q × V) | current (Q × I) |
| Name | acceptor | rejector |

## 5. Where you meet it

- **Radio tuning:** turning the dial changes C so f0 matches one station; high Q rejects neighbours. Your FM radio and Wi-Fi front-ends both do this.
- **LC oscillators** (Part 6) use a tank circuit to set the frequency.
- **Filters** that pass or block one band.

## Traps

- Series → impedance **minimum**; parallel → impedance **maximum**. Swapping these is the most common resonance error.
- Both have **unity** power factor at resonance.
- f0 doesn't depend on R; Q and bandwidth do.
