## 1. Voltage amplifiers vs power amplifiers

Small-signal stages (lesson 29) raise **voltage**. The last stage must deliver real **power** to a speaker, motor or antenna: large current and voltage swings. Now **efficiency** matters — wasted power becomes heat in the transistors.

`Efficiency η = (AC power delivered to the load) / (DC power drawn from the supply)`

## 2. Classes by conduction angle

How much of each input cycle does the output transistor conduct?

| Class | Conducts | Max efficiency | Distortion | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | full 360° | **25%** (resistive load), **50%** (transformer-coupled) | lowest | small, high-fidelity stages |
| **B** | 180° (half) | **78.5%** (= π/4) | crossover distortion | push-pull audio output |
| **AB** | a little more than 180° | between A and B (≈ 50–78%) | low | almost all audio amplifiers |
| **C** | less than 180° | above 78.5%, up to ≈ 90%+ | very high — needs a tuned load | **RF transmitters** |
| D (bonus) | switching (PWM) | > 90% in practice | filtered out | modern audio, class-D speakers |

## 3. Class A

Biased with the Q-point in the middle of the load line, so it conducts all the time — even with no signal, full quiescent current flows and heat is wasted.

- With a resistor in the collector (series-fed): maximum η = **25%**.
- With a transformer coupling the load: the DC drop in R_C disappears and the swing doubles, so maximum η = **50%**.

## 4. Class B push-pull

Two transistors, each biased exactly at cut-off, each handling **one half** of the waveform:

- **Complementary-symmetry:** an NPN for the positive half and a PNP for the negative half, emitters joined at the output.
- **Transformer-coupled push-pull:** two identical transistors driving a centre-tapped output transformer.

No signal → no current → little waste.

**Derivation of 78.5%.** For each transistor conducting a half-sine of peak I_m from supply V_CC (full swing, V_m = V_CC):
- DC supply power: average current of a full-wave rectified sine is 2I_m/π, so `P_dc = V_CC × 2I_m/π`.
- AC load power: `P_ac = (V_m/√2)(I_m/√2) = V_CC I_m / 2`.
- `η = P_ac / P_dc = (V_CC I_m/2) / (2V_CC I_m/π) = π/4 ≈ 78.5%`.

**Crossover distortion.** Each transistor needs ≈ 0.7 V on its base before it conducts, so for small signals around zero **neither** conducts — a flat step appears where the waveform crosses zero.

## 5. Class AB — the cure

Bias both transistors **just** into conduction — a small quiescent current — so one takes over smoothly as the other turns off. Usual biasing: **two diodes** (matching the two V_BE drops, and tracking their temperature) or a **V_BE multiplier** between the bases. Efficiency is slightly below class B; crossover distortion disappears.

## 6. Class C and tuned amplifiers

The transistor conducts only short pulses (much less than half a cycle) — highly distorted, but very efficient. A **tuned LC tank** as the load "rings" at its resonant frequency f₀ = 1/(2π√LC) and restores a clean sine wave ([lesson 20](/learning-guide/nec/electronics/20-resonance/)). That's why class C is used in **radio-frequency transmitters**, at a single frequency.

A **tuned amplifier** in general uses an LC tank as the load so it amplifies a narrow band around f₀ (bandwidth f₀/Q) — radio IF and RF stages. **Single-tuned** has one tank; **double-tuned** couples two for a flatter, wider pass band.

## 7. Power transistors and heat

- Power BJTs (and MOSFETs) have large junction areas, lower β, and metal tabs for mounting.
- Heat flows from junction to case to heat sink to air; each step has a **thermal resistance** (°C/W). Junction temperature = ambient + power × total thermal resistance.
- Ratings are **derated** at high temperature. Thermal runaway (lesson 28) is the main danger.

## Lok Sewa: write it (10 marks · 16 minutes)

**Question.** Explain the class B push-pull amplifier. Show that its maximum efficiency is 78.5%. What is crossover distortion and how is it removed?

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**Circuit.** Two transistors operated in class B (biased at cut-off): in complementary-symmetry form, an NPN and a PNP with joined emitters driving the load; or two NPNs driving a centre-tapped output transformer. *(Draw.)*

**Working.** During the positive half-cycle, Q1 conducts and supplies current to the load; Q2 is off. During the negative half-cycle, Q2 conducts and Q1 is off. The two halves combine in the load to form the full waveform. With no signal, neither transistor conducts, so standby dissipation is very low.

**Efficiency.** With peak load current I_m and full swing V_m = V_CC:
- P_dc = V_CC × (2I_m/π)
- P_ac = V_m I_m/2 = V_CC I_m/2
- η = P_ac/P_dc = π/4 = **78.5%** (maximum).

**Crossover distortion.** Each transistor needs about 0.7 V of V_BE before it conducts, so near the zero crossing neither conducts and the output has a dead zone. *(Sketch the distorted waveform.)*

**Remedy — class AB.** Apply a small forward bias to both bases (two diodes or a V_BE multiplier) so each transistor conducts slightly more than 180°; the hand-over becomes smooth. Diodes also track temperature, preventing thermal runaway.

**Advantages.** High efficiency, low standby power, no DC in the load (complementary). **Applications.** Audio power output stages.

</details>
