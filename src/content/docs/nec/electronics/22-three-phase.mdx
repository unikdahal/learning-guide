## 1. What it is

Put **three** coils on a generator, spaced 120° apart around the shaft. As it spins, each coil produces its own sine wave, each **120° behind the previous**. In Nepal and India the three phases are usually named **R, Y, B** (red, yellow, blue); the order they peak in, R → Y → B, is the **phase sequence**.

At every instant, the three balanced voltages (or currents) **add up to zero**.

## 2. Two ways to connect three windings

The shapes are the same as [star and delta](/learning-guide/nec/electronics/06-star-delta/) in lesson 6.

**Vocabulary:** a **phase** quantity is across or through one winding; a **line** quantity is between two supply lines or in one line.

### Star (Y)

One end of each winding joins at a common **neutral** point.

- `VL = √3 × Vph`  (line voltage is √3 times phase voltage; line voltage leads by 30°)
- `IL = Iph`
- Gives two voltages from one supply: Nepal's **400 V** between lines and **230 V** from line to neutral (400/√3 ≈ 230).
- The neutral carries the imbalance; in a perfectly balanced load it carries **zero** current.

### Delta (Δ)

Windings are connected end to end in a loop; no neutral.

- `VL = Vph`
- `IL = √3 × Iph`

| | Star | Delta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voltage | VL = √3 Vph | VL = Vph |
| Current | IL = Iph | IL = √3 Iph |
| Neutral | yes | no |

## 3. Power

For a balanced load, either connection:

`P = √3 VL IL cos φ`  (= 3 × Vph Iph cos φ)

Similarly `Q = √3 VL IL sin φ` and `S = √3 VL IL`.

**Worked example.** A balanced star load on 400 V draws 10 A per line at pf 0.8.
P = 1.732 × 400 × 10 × 0.8 ≈ **5.54 kW**. Phase voltage = 400/√3 ≈ **231 V**.

**Worked example.** A delta-connected motor on 400 V takes 17.32 A line current. Phase current = 17.32/1.732 = **10 A**; each winding sees the full **400 V**.

## 4. Why three-phase

1. **Constant total power.** In single-phase, power pulses 100 times a second (at 50 Hz). In balanced three-phase, the three pulses overlap so the **total is constant** — motors run smoothly.
2. **Less copper.** For the same power and voltage, three-phase lines need less conductor material than single-phase.
3. **Self-starting motors.** Three-phase currents create a rotating magnetic field, so induction motors start on their own.
4. **Two voltages from one system** (star: 400 V and 230 V).

Motors are often started in star (lower voltage per winding, lower starting current) and then switched to delta to run — the **star–delta starter**.

## Traps

- **120°**, not 90° or 180°.
- Star: **voltage** has the √3; delta: **current** has the √3.
- Power uses **line** values with √3, or **phase** values with 3.

**Star (Y) connection.** One end of each of the three phase windings is joined to a common neutral; the other ends go to the lines. **Delta (Δ):** the windings are connected end-to-end in a closed loop and the lines are taken from the junctions. *(Draw both.)*

**Proof: V_L = √3 V_ph in star.** The line voltage between R and Y is the phasor difference of the phase voltages: `V_RY = V_R − V_Y`. V_R and V_Y are equal in magnitude (V_ph) and 120° apart, so −V_Y is 60° from V_R. Adding V_R and −V_Y by the parallelogram rule gives a resultant along the bisector:
`V_RY = 2 V_ph cos 30° = 2 V_ph × (√3/2) = √3 V_ph`, leading V_R by 30°. *(Draw the phasor diagram.)*

**Comparison.**

| | Star | Delta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voltage | V_L = √3 V_ph | V_L = V_ph |
| Current | I_L = I_ph | I_L = √3 I_ph |
| Neutral | available (3-phase 4-wire) | none |
| Voltages available | two (e.g. 400 V and 230 V) | one |
| Winding insulation | for V_L/√3 — cheaper | for full V_L |
| Use | distribution, transmission | motors, industrial loads |

**Power.** For either connection: `P = √3 V_L I_L cos φ`.

**Example.** Star, V_ph = 230 V → V_L ≈ 400 V; a 10 A, pf 0.8 balanced load takes P = √3 × 400 × 10 × 0.8 ≈ 5.54 kW.
