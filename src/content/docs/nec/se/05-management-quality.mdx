## 1. Project management

Activities: **planning** (scope, estimates, schedule, resources), **scheduling** (work breakdown structure, **Gantt charts**, activity networks — critical path, covered in Chapter 10), **risk management**, **people management**, **monitoring and control**, reporting. The "4 Ps": **people, product, process, project**. Project failure usually comes from unrealistic deadlines, changing requirements, under-estimation and poor communication.

## 2. Size and cost estimation

**Size metrics:**
- **LOC/KLOC** — lines of code: easy to count after the fact, language-dependent, hard to estimate early.
- **Function points (FP)** — measure functionality from the user's view, independent of language.

### Function points — worked

Count the five components and weight them (simple / average / complex):

| Component | Weights (S / A / C) | Count | Average weight | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| External inputs (EI) | 3 / 4 / 6 | 24 | 4 | 96 |
| External outputs (EO) | 4 / 5 / 7 | 16 | 5 | 80 |
| External inquiries (EQ) | 3 / 4 / 6 | 22 | 4 | 88 |
| Internal logical files (ILF) | 7 / 10 / 15 | 4 | 10 | 40 |
| External interface files (EIF) | 5 / 7 / 10 | 2 | 7 | 14 |
| **Unadjusted FP (UFP)** | | | | **318** |

**Value adjustment factor:** rate 14 general system characteristics (data communications, performance, reusability …) from 0 to 5; sum = ΣFᵢ. **VAF = 0.65 + 0.01 × ΣFᵢ**; **FP = UFP × VAF**.
With ΣFᵢ = 42: VAF = 1.07 → **FP = 318 × 1.07 ≈ 340**. (ΣFᵢ = 30 → 0.95 → ≈ 302.)

### Basic COCOMO (Boehm)

**Effort E = a × (KLOC)^b person-months; development time D = c × E^d months; average staff = E / D.**

| Mode | Project | a | b | c | d |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Organic** | small team, familiar, flexible requirements | 2.4 | 1.05 | 2.5 | 0.38 |
| **Semi-detached** | medium, mixed experience | 3.0 | 1.12 | 2.5 | 0.35 |
| **Embedded** | tight hardware/regulatory constraints | 3.6 | 1.20 | 2.5 | 0.32 |

**Worked: 32 KLOC.**

| Mode | Effort (PM) | Duration (months) | Staff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Organic | 2.4 × 32^1.05 ≈ **91.3** | 2.5 × 91.3^0.38 ≈ **13.9** | ≈ **6.6** |
| Semi-detached | 3.0 × 32^1.12 ≈ **145.5** | 2.5 × 145.5^0.35 ≈ **14.3** | ≈ 10.2 |
| Embedded | 3.6 × 32^1.20 ≈ **230.4** | 2.5 × 230.4^0.32 ≈ **14.3** | ≈ 16.2 |

Notice: duration barely changes while effort doubles — adding people doesn't shorten schedules proportionally (**Brooks's law**: adding people to a late project makes it later). **Productivity** = KLOC / E (organic: 32 / 91.3 ≈ 0.35 KLOC per person-month).

**Intermediate COCOMO** multiplies effort by an **effort adjustment factor** from 15 cost drivers (product, hardware, personnel, project attributes). **Detailed COCOMO** applies drivers per phase. **COCOMO II** has application-composition, early-design and post-architecture models and uses object points/FP.

Other techniques: expert judgement, estimation by analogy, Delphi, top-down and bottom-up estimation, **Putnam/SLIM**.

## 3. Risk management

**Risk** = an uncertain event that may harm the project. Types: **project** (schedule, resources), **product** (quality, performance), **business** (market, funding).
Process: **identify → analyse** (probability × impact = **risk exposure**) **→ plan** (avoid, minimise, contingency) **→ monitor**. Example: "key developer leaves" (probability 30%, impact high) → mitigate by pair programming and documentation. The **spiral model** builds risk analysis into every loop.

## 4. Software quality assurance

**Quality** = conformance to explicit requirements, standards and implicit expectations. **SQA** = the planned, systematic activities that give confidence the product meets quality requirements; separate from (and broader than) testing.

**SQA activities:** prepare an **SQA plan**; participate in process description; review engineering activities and work products; audit; ensure deviations are documented and handled; record non-compliance.

**SQA plan contents:** purpose and scope; management (organisation, tasks, responsibilities); documentation; **standards, practices, conventions and metrics**; reviews and audits; testing; problem reporting and corrective action; tools and methods; configuration management; records; training; risk management.

**Formal technical reviews (FTR):** **walkthroughs** (the author leads peers through the work; informal) and **inspections** (formal, with roles — moderator, author, reader, recorder, inspectors — checklists, and metrics). Typically 3–5 people, under 2 hours, review the **product, not the producer**. Cheapest way to find defects early.

**Statistical SQA:** collect and categorise defects, trace each to its cause (incomplete specification, logic error, standards violation …), apply the **Pareto principle** (80% of defects come from 20% of causes), fix the vital few causes. **Six Sigma** (3.4 defects per million; DMAIC — define, measure, analyse, improve, control).

**Software metrics:** product (size, complexity — cyclomatic, coupling, cohesion), process (defect removal efficiency, effort variance), project (cost, schedule). **Reliability:** MTTF, MTTR, **MTBF = MTTF + MTTR**; **availability = MTTF / (MTTF + MTTR)** — MTTF 990 h and MTTR 10 h → **99%**.

## 5. Standards and maturity models

**ISO 9001** — requirements for a **quality management system** (any industry); ISO 9000-3 / ISO 90003 interprets it for software. Certification by external audit. **ISO/IEC 25010** (formerly 9126) — product quality model. **ISO/IEC 12207** — software life-cycle processes.

**CMMI / SEI-CMM** (Software Engineering Institute, Carnegie Mellon) — process maturity in five levels:

| Level | Name | Characteristic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Initial** | ad hoc, chaotic; success depends on heroes |
| 2 | **Managed** (CMM: Repeatable) | basic project management: requirements management, planning, tracking, configuration management |
| 3 | **Defined** | organisation-wide standard process, tailored per project; training; peer reviews |
| 4 | **Quantitatively managed** | process and quality measured and controlled statistically |
| 5 | **Optimising** | continuous process improvement, defect prevention, innovation |

ISO 9001 vs CMMI: ISO certifies a minimum quality system (pass/fail); CMMI grades maturity in levels and is software-focused.

## 6. Configuration management

**Software configuration management (SCM)** controls change across the life cycle so that the product stays consistent.
- **Software configuration items (SCIs)** — anything under control: SRS, design, code, test cases, manuals.
- **Baseline** — a reviewed, approved version of SCIs that can change only through formal change control.
- **Version control** — track versions and branches (Git, SVN); **build/system building**.
- **Change management** — change request → impact analysis by the **change control board (CCB)** → approve/reject → implement → verify → update baseline.
- **Release management** — plan, package and deliver releases (versions, release notes, installation).
- **Configuration audit** and **status accounting** (report what changed, by whom, when).

## 7. Maintenance, re-engineering and formal methods

**Maintenance types:**
- **Corrective** — fix faults found in use.
- **Adaptive** — adapt to a changed environment (new OS, new tax law, new database).
- **Perfective** — add features, improve performance or usability on request (usually the largest share).
- **Preventive** — restructure to prevent future problems (refactoring, documentation).

**Lehman's laws** — continuing change, increasing complexity. Maintenance is typically **60–80%** of lifetime cost.

**Re-engineering** of legacy systems: source-code translation, **reverse engineering** (recover design from code), program restructuring, modularisation, data re-engineering. **Forward engineering** builds the new system from the recovered design.

**Formal methods** — mathematically based specification and verification (**Z notation**, VDM, B, model checking). Remove ambiguity and allow proofs; costly and need expertise, so used mainly for safety-critical systems (railway signalling, avionics). **Cleanroom software engineering** — formal specification, correctness verification instead of unit testing, and statistical usage testing.

## Lok Sewa: write it (10 marks · 16 minutes)

**Question.** Explain the basic COCOMO model. Estimate effort, development time and staff for a 32 KLOC organic project. Describe the CMMI maturity levels.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

**COCOMO** (Constructive Cost Model, Boehm 1981) is an algorithmic cost-estimation model that predicts effort and schedule from program size in KLOC. Basic COCOMO: effort E = a(KLOC)^b person-months; development time D = c(E)^d months; staff = E/D. Projects are classified as organic (a = 2.4, b = 1.05, c = 2.5, d = 0.38), semi-detached (3.0, 1.12, 2.5, 0.35) or embedded (3.6, 1.20, 2.5, 0.32). Intermediate COCOMO multiplies effort by an adjustment factor from 15 cost drivers; detailed COCOMO applies them per phase.

**Estimate (32 KLOC, organic).**
- E = 2.4 × 32^1.05 ≈ **91.3 person-months**
- D = 2.5 × 91.3^0.38 ≈ **13.9 months**
- Staff = 91.3 / 13.9 ≈ **6.6 ≈ 7 persons**
- Productivity = 32,000 / 91.3 ≈ 350 LOC per person-month.

**CMMI maturity levels.** *(Draw a staircase.)*
1. **Initial:** unpredictable, poorly controlled, reactive processes.
2. **Managed:** projects planned and tracked; requirements and configuration managed; earlier successes can be repeated.
3. **Defined:** standard organisational processes, tailored for each project; training and peer reviews.
4. **Quantitatively managed:** processes measured and controlled using statistical techniques; quantitative quality goals.
5. **Optimising:** continuous improvement through innovation and defect prevention.

Each level builds on the previous one; organisations are appraised against the process areas of each level.

</details>
