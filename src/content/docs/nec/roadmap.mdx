**Version 2, decided 13 Nov 2026.** Study everything NEC and Lok Sewa Computer Engineer share **at Lok Sewa depth** until **Sun 20 Dec**. Then spend **21 Dec – 3 Jan** on NEC-only topics and multiple-choice mocks. The exam window opens **Mon 4 Jan**; your exact day is on the admit card.

## Why this order, and the four guardrails

**Why it works.** NEC chapters 1–9 are almost all on the Computer Engineer syllabus ([map](/learning-guide/lok-sewa/computer-engineer/)). Learning them once, deeply, with short written answers, serves both exams. You won't have to re-learn them in January. Chapter 10 (drawing, economics, the NEC Act) is pure memorisation and NEC-only, so it's best learned **close to the exam**, before you forget it.

**Why "Lok Sewa study is enough for the licence" isn't quite true.** These NEC topics aren't in Lok Sewa, together worth roughly 15–20 of NEC's 100 marks:
- Chapter 10, about 6–10 marks.
- VHDL and RTOS details.
- Larman-style object-oriented design (GRASP patterns, visibility).
- C/C++ syntax traps.

NEC is also **multiple-choice under time pressure**, which is a different skill from writing answers.

**Guardrails:**

1. **The NEC model paper diagnostic stays on Sat 21 Nov**, so you know your baseline early.
2. **Every lesson ends with multiple-choice questions**, so practising the NEC format never stops.
3. **Mid-point check, Sat 12 Dec:** if Mock 2 is below 50, move Chapter 10 forward to Sat 19 Dec and drop the Sunday writing in Week 5.
4. **No Lok Sewa-only topics before NEC.** Compiler design, communications, emerging tech, technical writing and case studies all wait until January. The only exception is 30 minutes of general awareness on Sundays.

<Aside type="caution" title="Why NEC still can't be risked">
Whether the Computer Engineer post needs the NEC licence (and by when) is still unconfirmed. Until it is, treat passing NEC in January as part of the Lok Sewa plan, not a side task.
</Aside>

## Daily shape (unchanged)

| Time | Block | What goes here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lunch break | 15 min | 20 MCQs on the current topic, on your phone |
| 21:00–21:45 | No study | Eat, shower, sit down |
| 21:45–23:15 | **A — deep work** (90 min) | New theory, numericals, written answers |
| 23:30–00:45 | **B — recall** (75 min) | MCQs, flashcards, error log |
| By 01:45 | Lights out | Same time every night |

**Friday:** Block B only (45 min) or a night off. **Saturday:** about 6.5 h (12:00–14:30, 16:00–18:00, 21:30–23:30). **Sunday:** about 4.5 h, evening off. Roughly 22 h a week.

**"Write"** in the tables means one Lok Sewa Paper II answer, by hand, timed: 8 minutes for 5 marks, 16 for 10. Self-mark it (definition 2, structure 1, diagram 2, depth 3, example 1, conclusion 1) and rewrite anything under 6/10 within two days.

<Aside type="tip" title="Bad day rule">
Late office, rain on the ride, headache: 45 minutes of Block A or 25 MCQs, then sleep. Two bad days in a row: drop the next Saturday's evening block. Don't repay hours.
</Aside>

## What this gives you in January

By 4 January you'll have done all of these:
- Learnt Lok Sewa CE units 1–4 and 6–10 at written depth.
- Written about 10 Paper II answers.
- Started a general-awareness habit.

January can then go to what NEC never covered:
- Electronic communications (unit 5).
- Compiler design (13).
- Emerging tech (15).
- Theory of computation, graphics and AI in written form (11, 12, 14).
- Technical writing and case studies (Section D).
- General awareness and reasoning.

## Weekly review (Sunday, 15 min)

Log hours, lessons or sub-chapters finished, the week's test score, and error-log entries added and cleared. If a score is flat for two weeks, change the method (more MCQs, more repasses), not the hours.
