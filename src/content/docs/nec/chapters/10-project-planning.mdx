## 10.1 Engineering drawing

**Syllabus:** standard drawing sheets, dimensions, scale, line diagrams, orthographic and isometric projection, pictorial views, sectional drawings.

**Know cold**

- **ISO A-series sheets** (mm): A0 841 × 1189 (area 1 m²), A1 594 × 841, A2 420 × 594, **A3 297 × 420**, A4 210 × 297. Each size is half the one above; the ratio is 1 : √2.
- The title block sits at the bottom right.
- Lines: continuous thick = visible outlines; continuous thin = dimension, extension, leader and hatching lines; dashed = hidden edges; thin chain = centre lines; chain thick at the ends = cutting planes.
- Scales: full 1 : 1, reducing (1 : 2, 1 : 100), enlarging (2 : 1).
- **First-angle projection:** the object sits between the viewer and the plane; the top view is drawn **below** the front view; the side view from the left goes on the right. **Third-angle:** the plane sits between viewer and object; the top view goes **above** the front view. Each has a standard truncated-cone symbol — learn to tell them apart.
- **Isometric:** the three axes are 120° apart (30° to the horizontal). An isometric *projection* uses the isometric scale (about 0.82 of true length); an isometric *view/drawing* uses true lengths.
- Sectional views: full, half, offset, revolved; hatching at 45°.

## 10.2 Engineering economics

**Syllabus:** project cash flow, discount rate, interest and time value of money; discounted payback, NPV, IRR, MARR; comparing alternatives; depreciation and taxation in Nepal.

**Know cold**

- Compound interest: `F = P(1 + i)^n`; present worth `P = F / (1 + i)^n`. Effective annual rate `= (1 + r/m)^m − 1`.
- **NPV** = sum of discounted cash flows minus the initial investment. Accept if NPV > 0.
- **IRR** = the discount rate that makes NPV = 0. Accept if IRR ≥ MARR.
- **MARR** = minimum attractive rate of return, the hurdle rate set by the cost of capital and risk.
- Payback period ignores the time value of money; discounted payback doesn't. Benefit–cost ratio > 1 → accept.
- Comparing alternatives: equal lives → present worth; unequal lives → annual worth (or the LCM of the lives); incremental analysis between pairs.
- **Depreciation methods:**

  | Method | Annual charge | Pattern |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Straight line | `(P − S) / n` | constant |
  | Declining (diminishing) balance | fixed % of book value | falls each year |
  | Sum of years' digits | `(n − t + 1) / SYD × (P − S)`, `SYD = n(n + 1)/2` | falls each year |
  | Sinking fund | annual deposit plus interest | **rises each year** |

- **Nepal taxation:** the Income Tax Act, 2058 sets depreciation by asset blocks using the diminishing-balance method: Block A 5% (buildings), B 25% (computers, furniture), C 20% (vehicles), D 15% (plant and machinery); Block E (intangibles) uses cost divided by useful life. Rates are in Schedule 2; annual Finance Acts change tax rates.

## 10.3 Project planning and scheduling

**Syllabus:** project classification, life-cycle phases, planning process, bar charts, CPM, PERT, resource levelling and smoothing, monitoring, evaluation and control.

**Know cold**

- Life cycle: concept/initiation → planning and design → execution → monitoring and control → closure.
- **Bar (Gantt) chart:** simple, but shows no dependencies or critical path.
- **CPM** (deterministic durations): the forward pass gives earliest start and finish (ES, EF); the backward pass gives latest (LS, LF). Total float `= LS − ES = LF − EF`. Free float `= ES of successor − EF`. The **critical path** has zero float and is the longest path.
- **PERT** (probabilistic, beta distribution): expected time `te = (a + 4m + b) / 6`; `σ = (b − a) / 6`. Project σ = √(sum of variances on the critical path). `Z = (T − Te) / σ` gives the probability of finishing by T.
- **Resource levelling:** caps resource use, and may **extend** the project. **Resource smoothing:** evens resource use **within float, without extending** the duration.
- Crashing trades extra cost for shorter duration on critical activities.
- Earned value: CV = EV − AC, SV = EV − PV, CPI = EV/AC, SPI = EV/PV.

## 10.4 Project management

**Syllabus:** information systems, project risk analysis and management, project financing, tendering and its process, contract management.

**Know cold**

- Risk process: identify → analyse (qualitative, quantitative) → respond (avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept) → monitor.
- Financing: equity, debt (loans, bonds), grants, BOT/BOOT, public–private partnership. **Capital structure** = the mix of debt and equity used to fund the project.
- Nepal public procurement is governed by the Public Procurement Act, 2063 and its Regulations; e-bidding runs on bolpatra.gov.np.
- Tender process: prequalification → bid documents → invitation → bid submission with bid security → bid opening → evaluation → letter of acceptance → performance security → contract agreement.
- **Contract types:** lump sum; item rate / unit rate (measured quantities); **cost-plus** (cost + percentage, cost + fixed fee, cost + incentive); turnkey / design–build; BOT. A lump-sum or item-rate contract is **not** cost-plus.
- Contract management: variations, claims, extensions of time, dispute resolution (negotiation, dispute board, arbitration).

## 10.5 Engineering professional practice

**Syllabus:** environment and society; professional ethics; regulatory environment; contemporary engineering issues; occupational health and safety; roles of the Nepal Engineers' Association (NEA).

**Know cold**

- Ethics: public safety first, honesty, competence, confidentiality, avoiding conflicts of interest, no bribery.
- **NEA** is the professional association (welfare and development of engineers). **NEC** is the statutory regulator (registration, recognition, conduct). Don't confuse them.
- OHS: hazard identification, risk assessment, PPE, safety training, accident reporting.
- Environmental studies before projects: IEE and EIA, under Nepal's environmental protection law.
- Societies by economic base: hunting–gathering, pastoral, horticultural, agrarian, industrial, post-industrial.

## 10.6 Engineering regulatory body — NEC Act and Regulations

**Syllabus:** the Nepal Engineering Council (Act and Regulations).

**Know cold**

- **Nepal Engineering Council Act, 2055** — royal seal and publication 2055-11-27 BS (March 1999); first amendment 2079. **Regulations, 2057** — amended 2064, 2069 and 2080 (the third amendment took effect 16 May 2023).
- No one may practise engineering in Nepal without registration with the Council; the Council also recognises engineering qualifications and educational institutions.
- NEC functions: registration of engineers, recognition of degrees and institutions, monitoring of colleges, professional code of conduct and discipline.
- Registration categories: General Registered Engineer, Professional Engineer, Non-Nepali Registered Engineer.
- Commonly quoted (verify in the Act): the Council has 21 members; the Registrar is the member-secretary.
