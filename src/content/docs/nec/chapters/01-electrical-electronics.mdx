## 1.1 Basic concepts

**Syllabus:** Ohm's law; voltage, current, power and energy; conducting and insulating materials; series and parallel circuits; star–delta and delta–star conversion; Kirchhoff's laws; linear and non-linear, bilateral and unilateral, active and passive circuits.

**Know cold**

- `V = IR`. `P = VI = I²R = V²/R`. Energy `W = Pt`; 1 kWh = 1 unit = 3.6 MJ.
- Resistance `R = ρL/A`: longer wire → more resistance, thicker wire → less. Good conductors: silver, copper, aluminium. Insulators: glass, mica, PVC, rubber.
- **Series:** same current, `R = R1 + R2 + …`, voltage divides in proportion to R. **Parallel:** same voltage, `1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + …`; two resistors give `R1R2/(R1 + R2)`; current divides inversely to R.
- **KCL:** currents into a node sum to zero (conservation of charge). **KVL:** voltages around a loop sum to zero (conservation of energy).
- **Delta → star:** `Ra = (Rab · Rca) / (Rab + Rbc + Rca)`. Equal resistors: `R_star = R_delta / 3`, so a 3 Ω delta becomes a 1 Ω star.
- **Star → delta:** `Rab = (RaRb + RbRc + RcRa) / Rc`. Equal resistors: `R_delta = 3 · R_star`.
- **Linear:** parameters don't change with voltage or current; superposition holds (R, L, C). **Non-linear:** diode, transistor.
- **Bilateral:** same behaviour in both directions (resistor). **Unilateral:** direction matters (diode).
- **Active elements** supply energy (voltage and current sources, transistors in the small-signal sense). **Passive elements** only absorb or store it: R, L, C.

## 1.2 Network theorems

**Syllabus:** superposition, Thevenin, Norton, maximum power transfer; RL, RC and RLC circuits; series and parallel resonance; active and reactive power.

**Know cold**

- **Superposition:** linear circuits only. One source at a time: voltage sources become shorts, current sources become opens. Doesn't apply to power directly.
- **Thevenin:** `Vth` = open-circuit voltage at the terminals; `Rth` = resistance seen with all independent sources killed.
- **Norton:** `In` = short-circuit current; `Rn = Rth`; `Vth = In · Rth`.
- **Maximum power transfer:** `RL = Rth` (DC) or `ZL = Zth*`, the complex conjugate (AC). `Pmax = Vth² / (4 Rth)`. Efficiency at that point is **50%**.
- Time constants: RC circuit `τ = RC`, RL circuit `τ = L/R`. After one τ a charging capacitor reaches 63.2%; after 5τ it is effectively done.
- Reactances: `XL = 2πfL` (rises with f), `XC = 1/(2πfC)` (falls with f). Inductor: current lags voltage by 90°. Capacitor: current leads by 90°.
- **Resonance:** `XL = XC`, `f0 = 1 / (2π√(LC))`, power factor = 1.
  - Series: impedance minimum (= R), current maximum, "acceptor" circuit, voltage magnification.
  - Parallel: impedance maximum, line current minimum, "rejector" circuit.
  - Quality factor (series) `Q = ω0L/R`; bandwidth `= f0/Q`. Higher Q → sharper, narrower.
- **Power in AC:** real `P = VI cos φ` (W), reactive `Q = VI sin φ` (VAR), apparent `S = VI` (VA), `S² = P² + Q²`. Power factor `cos φ = P/S`, **maximum value 1** (purely resistive).

## 1.3 AC fundamentals

**Syllabus:** generation of alternating voltage and current, equations and waveforms; average, peak and RMS values; three-phase systems.

**Know cold**

- A coil rotating in a magnetic field gives `e = Em sin ωt` (Faraday's law), with `ω = 2πf`. Frequency is cycles per second (Hz); time period `T = 1/f`.
- For a sine wave: `Vrms = Vm/√2 ≈ 0.707 Vm`; half-cycle average `Vavg = 2Vm/π ≈ 0.637 Vm`.
- **Form factor** = RMS/average = **1.11** (sine). **Peak (crest) factor** = peak/RMS = **1.414** (sine). Square wave: both are 1.
- Mains meters read RMS; 230 V RMS means about 325 V peak.
- **Three-phase:** three voltages 120° apart.
  - Star (Y): `VL = √3 Vph`, `IL = Iph`; has a neutral.
  - Delta (Δ): `VL = Vph`, `IL = √3 Iph`.
  - Total power `P = √3 VL IL cos φ` for either connection.
- Advantages of three-phase: constant power delivery, less conductor for the same power, self-starting motors.

## 1.4 Semiconductor devices

**Syllabus:** diode and its characteristics; BJT configurations and biasing; small and large signal models; MOSFET and CMOS working principles and applications.

**Know cold**

- **PN diode:** conducts in forward bias after the knee (cut-in) voltage, about **0.7 V for silicon, 0.3 V for germanium**. Reverse bias: tiny saturation current until breakdown (Zener or avalanche). A Zener diode in reverse breakdown is a voltage regulator.
- **Rectifiers** (input frequency f, peak Vm):

  | | Half-wave | Centre-tap full-wave | Bridge |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Ripple frequency | f | 2f | 2f |
  | Peak inverse voltage | Vm | 2Vm | Vm |
  | Max efficiency | 40.6% | 81.2% | 81.2% |
  | Ripple factor | 1.21 | 0.482 | 0.482 |

- **BJT currents:** `IE = IB + IC`; `α = IC/IE` (just under 1); `β = IC/IB` (often 50–300); `β = α/(1 − α)`.
- **Regions:** active (BE forward, CB reverse) → amplifier; saturation and cut-off → switch.
- **Configurations:**

  | | Common emitter | Common base | Common collector |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Current gain | high (β) | below 1 (α) | high (β + 1) |
  | Voltage gain | high | high | about 1 |
  | Input impedance | medium | low | high |
  | Output impedance | medium | high | low |
  | Phase shift | 180° | 0° | 0° |
  | Use | general amplifier | high frequency | buffer (emitter follower) |

  Every configuration has three terminals; one is common to input and output and names the configuration.
- **Biasing** sets the Q-point on the DC load line. Fixed bias is least stable; **voltage-divider bias is the most stable** and most used.
- Small-signal models (h-parameters, re model with `re ≈ 25 mV / IE`) handle small AC swings around the Q-point; large-signal models handle switching and power stages.
- **MOSFET:** voltage-controlled, very high input impedance (insulated gate). Enhancement type needs `VGS` beyond the threshold to form a channel; depletion type has a channel at `VGS = 0`. Saturation-region current `ID = (k/2)(VGS − Vt)²`.
- **CMOS:** an nMOS and a pMOS in complementary pairs. In a steady state one is always off, so static power is almost zero; power is spent mainly while switching. High noise margin and density → used in almost all digital ICs.

## 1.5 Signal generators

**Syllabus:** oscillator principles; RC, LC and crystal oscillators; waveform generators.

**Know cold**

- An oscillator is an amplifier with **positive feedback** and no input signal.
- **Barkhausen criterion:** loop gain `|Aβ| = 1` and total loop phase shift 0° (or 360°).
- **RC phase-shift:** three RC sections give 180°, the amplifier gives 180°; `f = 1 / (2πRC√6)`; amplifier gain must be at least 29. Audio range.
- **Wien bridge:** `f = 1 / (2πRC)`; gain of 3 needed; low-distortion audio oscillator.
- **LC oscillators** (radio frequencies): **Hartley** uses a tapped inductor (two inductors, one capacitor). **Colpitts** uses a tapped capacitor (two capacitors, one inductor). `f = 1 / (2π√(LC))` with the equivalent L or C.
- **Crystal oscillator:** quartz's piezoelectric effect gives a very high Q and the most stable frequency (clocks, microprocessors). It has a series resonance below its parallel resonance.
- **Waveform generators:** astable multivibrator (free-running square wave, no stable state), monostable (one stable state, one-shot pulse), **bistable** (two stable states — this is the flip-flop; an SR flip-flop is a bistable multivibrator). The 555 timer works in astable and monostable modes. A Schmitt trigger squares up slow edges using hysteresis.

## 1.6 Amplifiers

**Syllabus:** classification of output stages; class A, B and AB stages; biasing class AB; power BJTs; transformer-coupled push-pull; tuned amplifiers; op-amps.

**Know cold**

- **Classes by conduction angle** (how much of the input cycle the transistor conducts):

  | Class | Conduction | Max efficiency | Note |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | A | 360° | 25% (resistive load), 50% (transformer-coupled) | lowest distortion |
  | B | 180° | **78.5%** (π/4) | crossover distortion; used as push-pull |
  | AB | between 180° and 360° | between A and B | small bias removes crossover distortion |
  | C | under 180° | above 78.5% (up to about 90%) | needs a tuned LC load; RF transmitters |

- **Push-pull:** two transistors each amplify one half-cycle. Transformer coupling also matches the load impedance.
- **Class AB biasing:** two diodes (or a VBE multiplier) hold both transistors just at the edge of conduction.
- **Power BJTs:** large area, low β, need heat sinks; watch for thermal runaway.
- **Tuned amplifiers:** an LC tank as the load amplifies a narrow band around `f0` — radio IF and RF stages.
- **Ideal op-amp:** infinite open-loop gain, infinite input impedance, zero output impedance, infinite bandwidth, infinite CMRR.
  - Inverting gain `−Rf/Rin`. Non-inverting gain `1 + Rf/R1`. Voltage follower gain 1 (buffer).
  - Also: summing amplifier, integrator, differentiator, comparator.
  - `CMRR = Ad/Acm` (often in dB). Slew rate = maximum rate of change of the output (V/µs).
- **Decibels:** power gain `= 10 log10(P2/P1)` dB; voltage gain `= 20 log10(V2/V1)` dB (equal impedances). +3 dB ≈ double the power; +20 dB = 10× voltage.
