## 6.1 Finite automata

**Syllabus:** finite automata and finite state machines; DFA and NFA equivalence; minimisation; regular expressions and their equivalence with FA; pumping lemma for regular languages.

**Know cold**

- DFA = (Q, Σ, δ, q0, F); q0 is the **start (initial) state**. Moore machines output on states, Mealy machines on transitions.
- DFA and NFA (and ε-NFA) accept exactly the same languages. Subset construction can blow up to `2^n` DFA states.
- Minimisation merges equivalent states (partition refinement; Myhill–Nerode).
- **Limitation of FSMs:** finite memory, so they can't count without bound — `a^n b^n` and balanced brackets are not regular.
- Kleene's theorem: regular expressions ≡ finite automata.
- The **pumping lemma** is used to prove a language is **not** regular.
- Regular languages are closed under union, concatenation, star, complement and intersection.

## 6.2 Context-free languages

**Syllabus:** CFGs; derivation trees (leftmost, rightmost); parse trees; ambiguous grammars; CNF, GNF, BNF; pushdown automata; CFL–PDA equivalence; pumping lemma for CFLs; properties of CFLs.

**Know cold**

- CFG = (V, T, P, S). An ε-production `A → ε` lets a variable vanish (ε is the empty string).
- **Ambiguous:** some string has two parse trees (equivalently, two leftmost derivations).
- **CNF:** every rule is `A → BC` or `A → a`. **GNF:** every rule is `A → aα` (a terminal followed by variables). **BNF** is a notation for writing grammars (`::=`).
- **PDA** = finite automaton + stack; accepts exactly the CFLs. Deterministic PDAs are strictly weaker than non-deterministic ones.
- CFLs are closed under union, concatenation and star, but **not** under intersection or complement.

## 6.3 Turing machines

**Syllabus:** TM notation and acceptance; TM as recogniser, computer of functions and enumerator; multi-track, multi-tape and non-deterministic TMs; Church–Turing thesis; universal TM; computational complexity, time and space, intractability, reducibility.

**Know cold**

- A TM has an infinite read/write tape, so it has unbounded memory — its advantage over an FSM (and over a PDA's stack-only access).
- Multi-tape, multi-track and non-deterministic TMs all recognise the same languages as the basic TM (they may be faster).
- **Universal TM:** takes an encoded TM and its input and simulates it — a programmable computer.
- **Church–Turing thesis:** anything algorithmically computable is computable by a TM.
- The **halting problem is undecidable.**
- **Chomsky hierarchy:**

  | Type | Grammar | Machine |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0 | unrestricted | Turing machine |
  | 1 | context-sensitive | linear bounded automaton |
  | 2 | context-free | pushdown automaton |
  | 3 | regular | finite automaton |

- P = solvable in polynomial time; NP = verifiable in polynomial time; NP-complete = in NP and every NP problem reduces to it. Reducing A to B shows B is at least as hard as A.

## 6.4 Graphics hardware and software

**Syllabus:** overview of computer graphics; display technology; raster-scan architecture; vector displays; display processors; input and output devices; graphics software and standards.

**Know cold**

- **Raster scan:** the beam sweeps every line; the image is held in a **frame buffer**. Frame buffer size = resolution × bits per pixel (1024 × 768 × 24 bits ≈ 2.25 MB).
- **Random-scan (vector) display:** draws lines directly from a **display file** of commands, executed by the **display processor**; sharp lines, poor at filled areas.
- CRT: electron gun, focusing and deflection systems, phosphor (persistence). Also LCD, LED, plasma.
- Standards and APIs: GKS, PHIGS, OpenGL.

## 6.5 2D transformations and clipping

**Syllabus:** translation, rotation, scaling, reflection, shear; composite transformations; 2D viewing pipeline; window-to-viewport; Cohen–Sutherland and Liang–Barsky line clipping.

**Know cold**

- Homogeneous coordinates use 3 × 3 matrices so translation becomes a multiplication.
- Translation: `x' = x + tx`, `y' = y + ty`. Rotation about the origin: `x' = x cos θ − y sin θ`, `y' = x sin θ + y cos θ`. Scaling: `x' = sx · x`, `y' = sy · y`.
- Reflection about the x-axis negates y; about the y-axis negates x; about `y = x` swaps x and y.
- **Shear** keeps parallel lines parallel and preserves area, but **does not preserve angles or lengths**.
- **Composite transformation:** several transformations multiplied into one matrix; order matters (applied right to left). Rotating about a point = translate to origin, rotate, translate back.
- Viewing pipeline: modelling → world → viewing → normalised → device coordinates.
- **Cohen–Sutherland:** 4-bit region codes (top, bottom, right, left). Both codes 0000 → accept; bitwise AND not 0000 → reject; otherwise clip and repeat.
- **Liang–Barsky:** parametric form with `p` and `q` values; fewer intersection calculations, usually faster.

## 6.6 3D transformations and projections

**Syllabus:** 3D translation, rotation, scaling, reflection, shear; composite transformations; 3D viewing pipeline; orthographic, parallel and perspective projections.

**Know cold**

- 4 × 4 homogeneous matrices; rotation about the x, y or z axis.
- **Parallel projection** (projectors parallel): orthographic (front, top, side; axonometric — isometric, dimetric, trimetric) and oblique (cavalier, cabinet).
- **Perspective projection** (projectors meet at the centre of projection): farther objects look smaller, parallel lines converge at vanishing points; one-, two- and three-point perspective.
