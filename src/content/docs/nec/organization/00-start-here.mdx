This course teaches **NEC Chapter 4.1–4.3 (ACtE04: control and central processing units; computer arithmetic and the memory system; I/O organisation and multiprocessors)**. The same material is most of **Lok Sewa Computer Engineer unit 2 (architecture, organisation and microprocessors)** in Section A of both papers — so every hour here counts twice.

It assumes you have finished [Digital from zero](/learning-guide/nec/digital/00-start-here/) (or at least lessons 8–14): flip-flops, registers, the 8085 and DMA are used without re-explaining them.

<Aside type="note" title="Why this is your fast chapter">
You already think in caches, pipelines and instruction sets as a software engineer — the data platform you work on lives or dies by locality. What's new is the **exam vocabulary and numericals**: address splitting, AMAT, pipeline speed-up, Booth's algorithm, IEEE 754 encoding, and the textbook control-unit designs (the books are Morris Mano and William Stallings).
</Aside>

## How to study each lesson (40–50 minutes)

1. Read once, quickly.
2. Work every numbered example on paper before looking at the answer.
3. Play with the lab where there is one — predict the result first.
4. Write the Lok Sewa answer against the clock, then compare.
5. Do the MCQs; note every miss.
